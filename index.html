Tribute to A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

About A.P.J. Abdul Kalam

Avul Pakir Jainulabdeen Abdul Kalam (15 October 1931 – 27 July 2015) was an Indian aerospace scientist and politician who served as the 11th President of India from 2002 to 2007. He was born and raised in Rameswaram, Tamil Nadu, and studied physics and aerospace engineering.

He spent the next four decades as a scientist and science administrator, mainly at the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) and Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), and was intimately involved in India's civilian space program and military missile development efforts. He thus came to be known as the Missile Man of India for his work on the development of ballistic missile and launch vehicle technology.

Achievements

  • He played a pivotal role in India's Pokhran-II nuclear tests in 1998, which established India as a nuclear power.
  • He was awarded the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian honor, in 1997 for his contributions to science and engineering.
  • Kalam was also a prolific author, writing several books on science, technology, and his vision for India's future.
  • He was known for his humility and dedication to education, inspiring millions of young Indians to pursue careers in science and technology.
  • He played a key role in the development of India's first satellite launch vehicle, Aryabhata.
